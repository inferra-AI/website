Inferra compared with Vercel, Heroku, Render, Railway and Fly.io

Pick Inferra if you want APIs, background workers, Postgres, a cache and buckets built from GitHub and run for a flat monthly price, with previews for the branches you choose and a sandbox around every app. Pick Vercel for frontend frameworks and a global CDN, Heroku for a decade-mature add-on ecosystem, Render for a broad managed catalog, Railway for cheap usage-billed experiments, and Fly.io for VMs in many regions.

Every competitor fact on this page links to that company’s own pricing page or documentation, read on 10 October 2026. Where we could not confirm something on an official page, we say not verified instead of guessing. Inferra is in preview: custom domains and the hosted MCP server are not available yet. Where Inferra is different

  • A bill that can’t grow by itself. Plans are flat. At a plan limit the next push stops; nothing is added to the bill. Railway documents a comparable hard limit for compute that takes workloads offline; Vercel offers an optional production pause that takes effect after a few minutes.
  • A sandbox around every app, Free included. Every app runs in a gVisor sandbox, and each space is its own private network. Fly.io also names its isolation (Firecracker microVMs); the others describe it differently or not at all.
  • App, database, cache and buckets in one file, from one vendor. Declare Postgres, the cache and buckets in .inferra/org.yaml next to the app; only the apps bound to a service can connect to it. On Vercel and Fly.io some of these come from partners.
  • Previews on every plan. Each matched branch gets a running copy of your apps. Previews share their preview space’s Postgres and other services, kept apart from production. Render needs a Pro workspace for previews; Railway’s PR environments are comparable.
  • Long-running workers, unlike Vercel. An app with a start command and no port is a worker that keeps running. Heroku, Render, Railway and Fly.io run workers too; this is a difference from Vercel only.

Where others are stronger today

  • Maturity. Inferra is in preview. Heroku has run buildpacks, Procfiles and Review Apps for over a decade, and the others are generally available products.
  • Custom domains. Every competitor has them; Inferra’s are coming soon.
  • Regions and edge. Fly.io runs machines in many regions; Vercel serves from a global CDN.
  • Compliance. Render’s Pro plan includes SOC 2 and ISO 27001 reports. Inferra does not hold these certifications.
  • Database recovery depth. Heroku, Render and Fly.io document point-in-time recovery, rollback or high-availability options beyond Inferra’s 7-day backup window.
  • Docker and scale to zero. Render, Railway and Fly.io accept a Dockerfile when buildpacks don’t fit (Vercel’s Container Runtime is in Beta and must serve HTTP), and Fly.io, Railway and Vercel can stop idle workloads to cost less. Inferra builds with buildpacks only and charges the same each month.

Inferra vs Vercel

Choose Vercel for Next.js and other frontend frameworks, a global CDN and instant previews of your site. Choose Inferra for backends that run all the time: APIs, workers and their database in one place.

  • Functions have a maximum duration: 300 seconds on Hobby and 800 seconds on Pro, with an extended 1,800-second maximum in Beta (limits).
  • Vercel’s own guide says a background worker loop stops, because “the instance scales down after 5 minutes without HTTP traffic in production” (guide).
  • Postgres and Redis come from Marketplace partners such as Neon, Supabase and Upstash (storage).
  • Pro is $20 per developer seat; usage beyond the included credit is pay as you go. Spend Management can pause production deployments, checked every few minutes (pricing, spend management).

Inferra vs Heroku

Choose Heroku for the most mature git-push platform and a very large add-on marketplace. Choose Inferra for the same buildpack workflow with a flat price, built-in buckets, previews on every plan and a sandbox per app.

  • No free plan since 28 November 2022; Eco dynos sleep after 30 minutes without traffic (FAQ, Eco dynos).
  • Dynos are billed per dyno, prorated to the second, with Postgres and Key-Value Store as separately billed add-ons (billing, pricing).
  • No object storage of its own: “Use a storage solution like S3” (docs).
  • Web requests must send a first byte within 30 seconds (request timeout); worker dynos have no such limit.

Inferra vs Render

Choose Render for a broad managed catalog with point-in-time Postgres recovery and compliance reports. Choose Inferra for a single flat price that includes compute, and previews and sandboxing on every plan.

  • Workspace plans: Hobby free, Pro $25/month, Scale $499/month; each service instance is billed on top (plan change). We could not read per-instance prices from Render’s pricing page; check render.com/pricing.
  • Free web services spin down after 15 minutes idle; free Postgres expires after 30 days (free tier).
  • Preview environments need a Pro workspace, and copied datastores start without data (previews).
  • Background workers run continuously on paid instances (workers).

Inferra vs Railway

Choose Railway for cheap experiments billed by the second, a lasting free plan and Docker support. Choose Inferra for a fixed monthly price and a published sandbox model on every plan.

  • Free plan with $1 of monthly credit; Hobby $5/month includes $5 of usage and Pro $20/month per workspace includes $20; usage beyond that is about $20 per vCPU-month and $10 per GB-month of memory (plans).
  • Usage limits on every plan: at the compute hard limit, “all your workloads will be taken offline” (cost controls).
  • S3-compatible buckets with free egress (buckets) and PR environments that replicate services, networking and variables (PR environments). Railway’s docs don’t say whether database data is copied.
  • HTTP requests can run for up to 15 minutes while data keeps flowing (limits).

Inferra vs Fly.io

Choose Fly.io for VMs close to users in many regions, controlled through an API. Choose Inferra for a managed workflow where a push builds and deploys everything, with the database, cache and buckets included.

  • Pay as you go with no platform fee; the trial lasts 2 hours of runtime or 7 days (pricing).
  • Apps run in Firecracker microVMs (architecture). New apps default to stopping when idle; turn auto-stop off or keep a minimum of machines running for always-on capacity (autostop).
  • Managed Postgres starts at $38/month plus $0.28 per GB-month of storage; Redis comes from Upstash and object storage from Tigris (Postgres, Redis, Tigris).
  • Review apps per pull request are a GitHub Actions recipe that starts “a single application without other resources” (guide).

Inferra’s side

Inferra’s plans, limits and network rules are on the homepage; workers, previews and services in the org.yaml reference; isolation, backups and deletion on the security page. To try it, follow your first deployment.
