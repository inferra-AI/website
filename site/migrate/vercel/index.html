Migration guide · Vercel

Move your app from Vercel to Inferra.

For Next.js and other apps that run as a server. Keep your repo; add two files, move your secrets and database. Edge functions and routing rules need changes.

  • 5 steps
  • 2 files to add
  • Your Vercel app keeps running until you switch

Before you start Custom domains aren't available yet. This guide moves your app to an Inferra URL; switch only if that works for you.

→

On Inferra

.inferra/org.yaml
version: 2
apps:
  - folder: web
    branches: [main, feature/*]
services:
  postgres:
    - name: db
      storage: 10Gi
.inferra/main.yaml
apps:
  web:
    run:
      port: 3000
    health:
      path: /health
    services: [db]

Put your app in its own folder (here, web/). It gets its database address as DB_URL. Your server must listen on 0.0.0.0 and the PORT it's given, and answer /health. Inferra reads these two files, not vercel.json. Previews for feature/* branches use a .inferra/feature.yaml in that branch; start by copying main.yaml. Configuration reference

What changes

Three things work differently.

Your Next.js app

Runs as a normal Node server (next start). Keep your build and start scripts in package.json.

Rewrites and edge rules

Move routing into the app. vercel.json isn't read.

Serverless functions

Become routes in the server, or a background worker for long jobs.

The move

Five steps. Your Vercel app keeps running until the last one.

  1. Connect your repoInstall the Inferra GitHub App and pick the repo.
  2. Add the two filesCommit .inferra/org.yaml and .inferra/main.yaml. Push to main.
  3. Set your secretsCommit only the names. Add the values in the dashboard.
  4. Copy your databaseTest a restore and check row counts. Then pause writes, take the final pg_dump and restore it from an app bound to db (Postgres is private).
  5. Switch your trafficKeep writes frozen on Vercel from the final copy until users are on the new URL, and stop its workers and schedulers. Check pages, sessions, jobs and data, then switch. To roll back after new writes, copy that data back first.
Copy the databaseexample
# 1. On a machine that can reach your current database
pg_dump --format=custom --no-owner --no-acl "$SOURCE_DATABASE_URL" > app.dump

# 2. In an Inferra app bound to the database, with app.dump copied there
pg_restore --no-owner --no-acl --exit-on-error --dbname="$DB_URL" app.dump

Postgres on Inferra is private, so run the restore from an app bound to the database. Move the dump over a route you control and never commit it. Check the Postgres version and extensions match before the final copy.
